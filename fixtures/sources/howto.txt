How to brew a better cup of coffee at home

You do not need an expensive machine to make coffee that tastes far better than a drip pot. You need
fresh beans, a decent grinder, a scale and a little attention to a few numbers.

Start with the ratio. A good place to begin is sixteen grams of water for every gram of coffee. For a
single mug that is about twenty grams of coffee to three hundred and twenty grams of water. Weigh both;
guessing by eye is where most home coffee goes wrong.

Grind just before you brew. Coffee goes stale fast once it is ground because more surface meets the
air. Aim for a texture like coarse sea salt for a pour over and finer for espresso. If the cup tastes
sour and thin, grind finer; if it tastes bitter and harsh, grind coarser.

Water temperature matters more than people think. Just off the boil, around ninety four degrees
Celsius, pulls out the sweetness without scorching. Pour in slow circles, let the grounds bloom for
thirty seconds first so trapped gas escapes, then finish the pour over the next two minutes.

Taste, then change one thing at a time. The whole craft is a feedback loop: adjust the grind, the
ratio or the temperature, taste again, and keep the version you liked.
